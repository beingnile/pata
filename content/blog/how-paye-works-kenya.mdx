Every month, before your salary hits your account, your employer sends a chunk of it to KRA. That chunk is PAYE — Pay As You Earn. It is Kenya's system for collecting income tax from employees.

Understanding how PAYE is calculated helps you:

- Verify your payslip is correct
- Spot errors from HR or payroll
- Know exactly how much tax you are paying
- Plan for tax reliefs that reduce your bill

## How PAYE Is Calculated

KRA uses a progressive tax system. The more you earn, the higher percentage you pay on the additional income. Not on all of it — just on the portion that falls into each bracket.

Here are the 2026 monthly brackets:

| Monthly Income (KSH) | Tax Rate |
|----------------------|----------|
| 0 - 24,000 | 10% |
| 24,001 - 32,333 | 25% |
| 32,334 - 500,000 | 30% |
| 500,001 - 800,000 | 32.5% |
| Above 800,000 | 35% |

This means someone earning KSH 100,000 does not pay 30% on the entire amount. They pay 10% on the first 24,000, 25% on the next 8,333, and 30% only on the remaining 67,667.

## Step-by-Step Example

**John earns KSH 80,000 per month.**

First, the tax is calculated on the gross amount before any deductions:

```
First 24,000 @ 10%      = 2,400
Next 8,333 @ 25%        = 2,083
Remaining 47,667 @ 30%  = 14,300

Gross tax               = 18,783
```

Then, personal relief is applied. Everyone gets this automatically:

```
Gross tax               = 18,783
Less: Personal Relief   = -2,400

Net PAYE                = 16,383
```

John pays **KSH 16,383** in PAYE every month.

If John paid KSH 3,000 per month in insurance premiums, he could claim insurance relief (a tax credit of 15% of premiums, up to KSH 5,000/month), reducing his PAYE further. Pension contributions work differently — they reduce his taxable income before the brackets are applied, which also lowers his PAYE.

## What Is Not Included in PAYE

PAYE is calculated on your **gross salary** before other statutory deductions like NSSF, SHIF, or Housing Levy. These are separate deductions that come out after PAYE is calculated.

However, voluntary pension contributions to a registered scheme **do** reduce your taxable income before PAYE is calculated. This is why maximizing pension contributions is one of the most effective ways to legally reduce your tax.

## Common PAYE Mistakes

**Mistake 1: Thinking the top rate applies to everything.**

If you earn KSH 600,000, you do not pay 32.5% on the entire amount. Only the portion above 500,000 is taxed at 32.5%. The rest is taxed at the lower brackets.

**Mistake 2: Not checking your payslip.**

Some employers make errors in tax calculations, especially after KRA updates brackets or reliefs. Always verify your PAYE matches what you expect.

**Mistake 3: Missing out on reliefs.**

Many employees are unaware of available deductions and reliefs. Insurance relief, mortgage interest deductions, and HOSP deductions can all significantly reduce your PAYE if claimed properly.

## The Bottom Line

PAYE is mandatory for all employees in Kenya. The system is progressive, so higher earners pay more — but only on the income above each bracket threshold. Personal relief and other reliefs reduce your tax bill. Understanding your bracket and available reliefs is the first step to keeping more of your salary.
