If you are a salaried employee in Kenya, you are probably overpaying your taxes. Not because KRA is cheating you — but because you are not using deductions and reliefs that the law allows.

There are two ways Kenyan tax law reduces your PAYE:

**Income deductions** reduce your taxable income *before* PAYE brackets are applied. Example: contributing KSH 20,000 to a pension scheme means you are only taxed on KSH 80,000 instead of KSH 100,000.

**Tax credits (reliefs)** reduce the tax *after* it has been calculated. Example: a KSH 2,400 personal relief is subtracted directly from your gross tax.

Both save you money. But they work differently. Here is what you need to know.

## 1. Personal Relief (Tax Credit — Automatic)

This is the only relief most people know about because it is applied automatically by your employer.

- **Amount:** KSH 2,400 per month (KSH 28,800 per year)
- **How to claim:** You do not need to do anything. Your employer applies this automatically.
- **Effect:** Reduces your calculated tax by KSH 2,400 every month.

This relief is available to every individual with a KRA PIN who is earning income.

## 2. Insurance Relief (Tax Credit)

If you pay premiums for **life insurance or education policies** with a registered Kenyan insurer, you can claim relief.

- **Amount:** 15% of premiums paid, up to KSH 5,000 per month
- **How to claim:** Provide your KRA PIN to your insurer. The insurer submits the information to KRA through the iTax system.
- **Effect:** Reduces your calculated tax by up to KSH 5,000 per month.

**Important:** SHIF contributions (2.75% of your salary) are a statutory deduction. They do **not** qualify for insurance relief. Only separate private insurance premiums do.

Many employees have private health insurance but do not provide their KRA PIN to the insurer. Without this, you miss out on the relief.

**Practical example:**

Sarah pays KSH 8,000 per month for a private health insurance policy. 15% of 8,000 is KSH 1,200. This is below the KSH 5,000 cap, so her entire premium qualifies for relief. Her PAYE drops by KSH 1,200 every month.

If she paid KSH 40,000 per month in premiums, 15% would be KSH 6,000. But she would only get relief on KSH 5,000 because that is the cap.

## 3. Pension Contribution Deduction (Income Deduction)

Contributions to a registered pension scheme or provident fund reduce your taxable income before PAYE is calculated.

- **Amount:** Deductible up to KSH 20,000 per month
- **How to claim:** Your employer deducts this before calculating PAYE, or you declare it on your annual iTax return.
- **Effect:** Your gross salary is reduced by the contribution amount before tax brackets are applied.

If your employer operates a pension scheme, check whether you are contributing the maximum allowed. Most employees contribute the bare minimum and miss out on tax savings.

**Practical example:**

Michael earns KSH 150,000 per month and contributes nothing extra to his pension. His tax calculation is based on the full KSH 150,000.

If he increases his voluntary pension contribution to KSH 20,000, his taxable income drops from KSH 150,000 to KSH 130,000. At the 30% tax bracket, this saves him approximately KSH 6,000 per month in PAYE. Plus, he is building retirement savings. Over a year, that is KSH 72,000 less tax.

## 4. Mortgage Interest Deduction (Income Deduction)

If you have a mortgage on your primary residence, the interest you pay reduces your taxable income.

- **Amount:** Deductible up to KSH 25,000 per month
- **How to claim:** Declare the interest on your annual iTax return and keep all mortgage statements.
- **Effect:** Reduces your taxable income by the interest amount before PAYE brackets are applied.

**This is not a tax credit.** It does not directly reduce your tax by KSH 25,000. Instead, it removes KSH 25,000 from the income that gets taxed. At the 30% bracket, a KSH 25,000 deduction saves you KSH 7,500 in PAYE.

This relief applies only to the **interest** portion of your mortgage payment, not the principal. You must be an owner-occupier — it does not apply to rental properties.

**Practical example:**

Peter pays KSH 45,000 per month in mortgage interest. He can claim a deduction on KSH 25,000 of this (the monthly cap). If he is in the 30% tax bracket, his taxable income is reduced by KSH 25,000, which saves him KSH 7,500 per month in PAYE.

To claim this, Peter must keep all his mortgage interest statements and declare them when filing his annual iTax return by June 30.

## 5. Home Ownership Savings Plan (HOSP) Deduction (Income Deduction)

If you are saving to buy or build your first home through an approved HOSP, those savings reduce your taxable income.

- **Amount:** Deductible up to KSH 8,000 per month
- **How to claim:** Open an HOSP with an approved institution (e.g., KCB, Co-operative Bank, Equity Bank) and declare the savings on your iTax return.
- **Effect:** Reduces your taxable income by the savings amount before PAYE brackets are applied.

**This is also not a tax credit.** KSH 8,000 deducted from your taxable income at the 30% bracket saves you KSH 2,400 per month in PAYE, not KSH 8,000.

HOSP is specifically for first-time homebuyers. You cannot use it if you already own a home.

**Practical example:**

Alice saves KSH 10,000 per month in an HOSP account. She can claim a deduction on KSH 8,000 (the cap). At the 30% bracket, this reduces her taxable income by KSH 8,000, which saves her KSH 2,400 per month in PAYE. Over a year, that is KSH 28,800 in tax savings.

## 6. Disability Deduction (Income Deduction)

If you are a person with disability and have a registered disability certificate, KSH 150,000 per month is deducted from your taxable income.

- **Amount:** KSH 150,000 per month (KSH 1.8 million per year)
- **How to claim:** Declare your disability status on iTax and provide your disability certificate number.
- **Effect:** Reduces your taxable income by KSH 150,000 before PAYE brackets are applied.

This is a substantial deduction. For many disabled employees earning below KSH 170,000, this effectively eliminates their PAYE entirely.

## How to Claim These

**During employment:**
- Give your KRA PIN to your private insurer for insurance relief.
- Ask your employer to increase pension deductions.
- If your employer offers a pension scheme, confirm they deduct your voluntary contributions *before* calculating PAYE.

**Annual filing (by June 30):**
- Declare mortgage interest, HOSP contributions, and disability status.
- Declare any pension contributions not already deducted by your employer.

**Keep records:**
- Retain all statements, receipts, and certificates for at least 5 years. KRA can audit you at any time.
