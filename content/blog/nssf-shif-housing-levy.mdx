Your payslip is a record of what you earned and what was taken away before the money reached your account. If you are formally employed in Kenya, there are at least four statutory deductions that appear every month:

1. PAYE (income tax)
2. NSSF (National Social Security Fund)
3. SHIF (Social Health Insurance Fund)
4. Affordable Housing Levy

PAYE gets most of the attention because it is usually the largest deduction. But the other three matter too — they fund your retirement, your healthcare, and national housing programs.

Let us break down each one.

## NSSF: Your Retirement Savings

The National Social Security Fund is Kenya's mandatory retirement savings scheme. Both you and your employer contribute.

### How NSSF Is Calculated

NSSF has two tiers:

**Tier I:** 6% of your salary, up to a maximum of KSH 540 per month (calculated on the first KSH 9,000 of your salary).

**Tier II:** 6% of your salary between KSH 9,001 and KSH 108,000, up to a maximum of KSH 5,940 per month.

**Combined maximum:** KSH 6,480 per month.

### Example

Grace earns KSH 60,000 per month:

```
Tier I:  6% of 9,000    = 540
Tier II: 6% of 51,000   = 3,060
Total NSSF:              3,600
```

James earns KSH 200,000 per month:

```
Tier I:  6% of 9,000    = 540
Tier II: 6% of 99,000   = 5,940
Total NSSF:              6,480
```

Even though James earns more than Grace, his NSSF contribution is capped at KSH 6,480 because the Tier II limit is based on earnings up to KSH 108,000 only.

### What Happens to Your NSSF Money

Your NSSF contributions accumulate in an individual account. When you reach retirement age (currently 60), you can access the funds as a lump sum or monthly pension. If you leave formal employment before retirement, you may be able to access a portion of your savings under certain conditions.

### Employer Contribution

Your employer matches your NSSF contribution. So if you contribute KSH 3,600, your employer also contributes KSH 3,600, for a total of KSH 7,200 going into your NSSF account each month.

## SHIF: Healthcare for Everyone

The Social Health Insurance Fund (SHIF) replaced NHIF in October 2024. It is Kenya's new national health insurance scheme.

### How SHIF Is Calculated

SHIF is straightforward: **2.75% of your gross monthly salary**.

There is a minimum contribution of KSH 300 per month, even if 2.75% of your salary would be lower.

### Example

Grace earns KSH 60,000:

```
SHIF: 2.75% of 60,000 = 1,650
```

John earns KSH 10,000:

```
SHIF: 2.75% of 10,000 = 275
Minimum: 300
Actual contribution: 300
```

### What SHIF Covers

SHIF provides access to a defined package of healthcare services at public and selected private facilities. The exact coverage details are still being rolled out, but the fund is intended to cover outpatient and inpatient services, maternity care, and certain chronic disease treatments.

### Employer Matching

Unlike NSSF, there is no employer matching for SHIF. The 2.75% comes entirely from your salary.

## Affordable Housing Levy

The Affordable Housing Levy was introduced in 2023 to fund the government's affordable housing program.

### How the Levy Is Calculated

It is simple: **1.5% of your gross monthly salary**.

There is no cap and no minimum.

### Example

Grace earns KSH 60,000:

```
Housing Levy: 1.5% of 60,000 = 900
```

James earns KSH 200,000:

```
Housing Levy: 1.5% of 200,000 = 3,000
```

### What the Levy Funds

The levy funds the construction of affordable housing units across Kenya. Employees who contribute may be eligible to apply for these housing units at subsidized rates, though the allocation process is still being developed.

### Employer Matching

Your employer matches your 1.5% contribution. So if you pay KSH 900, your employer also pays KSH 900, for a total of KSH 1,800 going to the housing fund.

## Complete Example: Grace's Payslip

Let us put it all together for Grace, who earns KSH 60,000 per month. Her PAYE is calculated from her gross salary (NSSF, SHIF, and Housing Levy do not reduce it), then all deductions are subtracted:

```
PAYE calculation:
First 24,000 @ 10%        = 2,400
Next 8,333 @ 25%          = 2,083
Remaining 27,667 @ 30%    = 8,300
────────────────────────────────
Gross tax                  12,783
Less personal relief      - 2,400
────────────────────────────────
Net PAYE                   10,383

Gross Salary:              60,000
Statutory Deductions:
  PAYE:                  -10,383
  NSSF (Tier I + II):     -3,600
  SHIF:                   -1,650
  Housing Levy:             -900
────────────────────────────────
Total Deductions:        -16,533

Net Salary:               43,467
```

Grace takes home KSH 43,467, which is 72.4% of her gross salary.

## What Changed in 2024-2026

Several changes have affected Kenyan payslips recently:

1. **SHIF replaced NHIF** (October 2024): The contribution structure changed from NHIF's fixed bands to SHIF's 2.75% of gross salary.

2. **Housing Levy introduced** (2023): A new 1.5% deduction for the affordable housing program.

3. **NSSF contribution rates increased** (2014, phased in): The Tier II maximum increased from KSH 360 to KSH 5,940 as part of the new NSSF Act.

4. **New PAYE brackets** (2024/2025): The top bracket increased from 30% to 32.5% and 35% for higher earners.

## Common Questions

**Q: Can I opt out of these deductions?**

A: No. NSSF, SHIF, and the Housing Levy are mandatory for all formally employed Kenyans. Only PAYE can be reduced through tax deductions and reliefs.

**Q: Do these deductions reduce my PAYE?**

A: No. SHIF and Housing Levy do not affect PAYE. NSSF may reduce your taxable income in some employer payroll systems, but in practice most employers calculate PAYE on gross salary first. The only deductions that consistently reduce PAYE are registered pension contributions and mortgage interest (claimed on your annual return).

**Q: What happens if my employer deducts but does not remit?**

A: This is illegal. Employers must remit all deductions to the respective authorities by the 9th of the following month. If you suspect non-remittance, check your NSSF and KRA statements online.

**Q: How do I verify my deductions?**

A:
- NSSF: Check your statement at nssf.or.ke
- KRA/PAYE: Log into your iTax account
- SHIF: Check your status on the SHA portal

## The Bottom Line

Your payslip deductions fund important national programs — retirement, healthcare, and housing. While you cannot avoid them, understanding them helps you verify accuracy and plan your finances better.

The largest portion of your deductions is usually PAYE, and that is where you have the most control. By claiming all available tax deductions and reliefs, you can reduce your PAYE and increase your net salary.
